1. THE HEADLINE: THE SINGLE-THRESHOLD PLAN WAS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

docs/implementation.md section 3.2 instructs: "The threshold goes BETWEEN the
lowest positive top-1 score and the highest negative top-1 score."

Measured on the real index, that is not achievable. There is no such value.

    Question                                      Set          top-1 cosine
    expense ratio of HDFC Large Cap Fund          answerable       0.816
    exit load on HDFC Flexi Cap Fund              answerable       0.764
    riskometer level HDFC Small Cap               answerable       0.625
    benchmark of HDFC Balanced Advantage          answerable       0.622
    lock-in period HDFC ELSS                      answerable       0.526
    minimum SIP amount                            answerable       0.445
    What is HDFC Bank's FD interest rate?         REFUSE           0.561  <--
    Which fund is best for my portfolio?          REFUSE           0.486  <--
    how to download capital gains statement       REFUSE (A6)      0.327
    Tell me about Bitcoin.                        REFUSE           0.164
    What is the weather in Mumbai?                REFUSE           0.153
    What is the price of gold today?              REFUSE           0.150

    COSINE GAP = 0.445 (lowest answerable) - 0.561 (highest refuse) = -0.116

Two questions we CANNOT answer outscore three we CAN. The two offenders are
exactly the finance-adjacent negatives. "What is the weather in Mumbai" is easy
to refuse; "What is HDFC Bank's FD interest rate?" is not, because:

  - every chunk embedding is prefixed with the scheme name, so any question
    containing "HDFC" gets a cosine boost against all 26 chunks
  - "interest rate" is semantically close to "expense ratio"
  - the index is small enough that 4 hits = 15% of the corpus

`cli ask "What is HDFC Bank's FD interest rate?" --explain` shows the whole
mechanism in one line:

    1. FAIL (coverage 0.078 < 0.55)  sim=0.561 cov=0.078
       [balanced_advantage / Fees, exit load and investment limits]
       matched: hdfc

It matched on the single token "hdfc" and nothing else.

This is why A2 was flagged as the highest-risk item in Phase 2. Phase 2's
evidence (correct answer scoring 0.166, hard negative 0.327) was a symptom of
this same collision, and it got worse as the question set grew.

2. THE FIX: A SECOND, ORTHOGONAL SIGNAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

docs/implementation.md section 3.2 anticipates this failure: "if the sets do not
separate, add the lexical signal from section 3.4 rather than lowering the bar
until A6 passes."

Implemented as src/rag/lexical.py -- IDF-weighted lexical coverage: the fraction
of the question's DISTINCTIVE vocabulary that the chunk actually contains.

    coverage = sum(idf(t) for t in query_terms if t in chunk) / sum(idf(t) for t in query_terms)
    idf(t)   = log((N+1)/(df(t)+1)) + 1

    Measured, same 12 questions:

    Signal        answerable min    refuse max      gap
    cosine              0.445          0.561      -0.116   FAILS
    lexical coverage    0.637          0.494      +0.143   WORKS

Why IDF rather than raw term counts: "fund", "HDFC" and "expense" appear across
many chunks and must not count for much; "riskometer" and "lock-in" appear in
one and must count for a lot. A raw-count overlap would call the FD question a
good match because both strings contain "HDFC". The IDF weighting is what makes
"hdfc" alone score 0.078 instead of 1.0.

No hand-maintained list of financial filler words. The ~40 stopwords are generic
English function and interrogative words only ("what", "which", "is", "of").
Domain words are deliberately NOT stopwords -- "lock-in" and "expense" must keep
their weight, and IDF already down-weights the common ones on its own.

BOTH gates must pass. Either alone is insufficient, and the two are genuinely
orthogonal: cosine finds the plausible chunk, coverage checks it is really about
what was asked.

3. PROPOSED VALUES -- NOT YET IN .env, AWAITING SIGN-OFF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  MIN_SIMILARITY        = 0.30
  MIN_LEXICAL_COVERAGE  = 0.55

  MIN_SIMILARITY is deliberately LOOSE. The lowest answerable score is 0.445,
  and 0.30 sits well under it, so the cosine gate never rejects a genuinely good
  chunk. It is a sanity floor, not the discriminator -- a loose gate that never
  fires is better than a tight one that causes silent false refusals.

  MIN_LEXICAL_COVERAGE is the gate doing the real work. 0.55 sits between the
  refuse maximum (0.494) and the answerable minimum (0.637), with roughly
  +0.06 margin on the refuse side and +0.09 on the answerable side.

  A grid search over coverage in [0.20, 0.85] x cosine in [0.20, 0.55] found 15
  valid operating points. The usable region is:

      coverage in {0.50, 0.55, 0.60}  AND  cosine in {0.20 ... 0.40}

  0.55 / 0.30 is the centre of that region. The thresholds are NOT tight: small
  corpus drift will not break them, which is what we want for a class demo.

Both are already the values in src/config.py defaults and in .env.example, so
nothing needs changing to accept them -- only the user's confirmation before
copying to .env.

tests/test_retriever.py::test_thresholds_have_a_separation_margin asserts that
min(answerable) > max(refuse) and that the configured value sits between them.
If a re-ingest changes the corpus, or someone lowers a threshold, the suite
fails instead of the refusal path degrading unnoticed.

4. src/rag/retriever.py                      229 lines  (NEW)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  class Retriever:
      __init__(cfg)               - embedder (imported, never re-instantiated),
                                    lexical gate, collection validation
      search(query, top_k,        - THE GATE. Returns only hits clearing both
             scheme_key, augment)    conditions, ranked, capped at top_k
      explain(query, top_k, ...)  - diagnostic: both signals + verdict + which
                                    terms matched, per candidate
      index_size                  - property

  Non-obvious decisions:

  - The gate is INSIDE search(). A gate that callers can forget is not a
    defence. This is architecture decision D5 and the reason search() is the
    only supported entry point.
  - _candidates() over-fetches (top_k * 3). Gating on a shallow top_k starves
    the result: a high-similarity / low-coverage chunk will otherwise crowd out
    a lower-similarity chunk that actually contains the answer. "benchmark of
    HDFC Balanced Advantage" hit exactly this case -- the correct chunk was
    ranked 8th on a top_k=4 over-fetch of 4.
  - The query is embedded with NO document prefix. The prefix describes the
    chunk, not the question. augment_query() may append a bare scheme_key
    (see section 7) but never "{scheme_name} - {section}".
  - Imports Embedder from src.ingest.embedder. A second SentenceTransformer is a
    silent retrieval bug the instant the two disagree on pooling or
    normalisation.
  - Two guard exceptions, IndexModelMismatch and EmptyIndexError, both caught in
    cli.py with the right exit code and a runnable next command.

  RANKING IS score x COVERAGE, NOT score ALONE. See section 6.

  The gate returns a reason string ("coverage 0.078 < 0.55") on every rejection.
  That is what makes a bad threshold debuggable instead of mysterious -- you can
  always answer "which gate fired, and on what number".

5. src/rag/lexical.py                        133 lines  (NEW, not in the plan)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  class LexicalGate:
      from_collection(cfg)  - builds IDF stats by reading the stored collection
      idf(term)
      coverage(question, chunk_embedding_text)
      matched_terms(question, chunk_embedding_text)   - why a hit failed
  tokenize(text) -> set[str]

  Added beyond the file list in implementation.md section 3.1. It is a distinct
  concern with its own testable surface, and folding it into retriever.py would
  have mixed vector search and string matching in one file.

  ONE SUBTLETY THAT MATTERS A LOT. from_collection() reconstructs each chunk's
  EMBEDDING text ("{scheme_name} - {section}\n{body}") and scores against that,
  not against the bare stored body. During tuning, scoring against the bare body
  dropped coverage to ~0.0 for every scheme-named question -- because the stored
  body does not contain the word "HDFC", only the prefix does. That made every
  answerable question look like a refusal and briefly suggested the whole
  approach was unsalvageable. The two signals must be computed over the SAME
  string, or the gate is incoherent.

  The reconstructed string is not persisted. It is rebuilt from metadata that
  IS persisted (scheme_name, section), so it stays correct across re-ingests
  without a schema change.

6. SECOND FINDING: RANKING BY COSINE ALONE WAS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For "benchmark of HDFC Balanced Advantage", top-1 was the FEES chunk, not the
BENCHMARK chunk. The two are nearly tied on cosine:

    Fees       0.622  coverage 0.695     <- won on cosine
    Benchmark  0.607  coverage 1.000     <- the right answer

Cause: every chunk of a fund is prefixed with that fund's name, so "HDFC
Balanced Advantage" matches the fees chunk, the benchmark chunk and the overview
chunk EQUALLY. Cosine cannot break that tie, and the one discriminating term
("benchmark") gets swamped. The fees chunk won on a coin flip.

Ranking by score x coverage fixes it, because the correct chunk has strictly
higher coverage. Effect on the eval set:

    top-1 correct, ranked by cosine ............ 5/6
    top-1 correct, ranked by score x coverage ... 6/6

IMPORTANT: Hit.score is left as the RAW COSINE value. Only the sort key is the
product. The thresholds are defined against cosine, so score must keep meaning
cosine or MIN_SIMILARITY becomes unfalsifiable. Do not "simplify" this by storing
the blended value in score.

7. THIRD FINDING: SECTION 3.4's SCHEME-KEY FIX DOES NOT HELP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Implementation.md section 3.4 says the cheap recall fix is to append the matching
scheme_key to the question text before embedding, and to log whether it helped.

It does not help. Measured, augment on vs off, top-1 cosine:

    question                                     off      on     delta
    expense ratio of HDFC Large Cap Fund       0.846   0.816   -0.029
    exit load on HDFC Flexi Cap Fund           0.794   0.764   -0.030
    benchmark of HDFC Balanced Advantage        0.625   0.622   -0.002
    riskometer level HDFC Small Cap            0.607   0.625   +0.018
    lock-in period HDFC ELSS                   0.520   0.526   +0.006
    minimum SIP amount                         0.445   0.445    0.000

Net effect zero, and slightly NEGATIVE on the two cleanest questions. It also
cannot fix the two colliding negatives, because those name no scheme and so have
nothing to append.

Implemented and measured, then left available behind the augment= flag but
DEFAULTING TO THE PLAIN QUESTION. The spec asked for it to be logged; this is
that log. Consequence: no cross-encoder reranker is warranted on current
evidence. Revisit only if the Phase 4 end-to-end eval shows real recall misses.

  0.55 gate -- and the reason is worth knowing. "mumbai" is a rare term in this

  manager's qualifications: "CA (ICAI, INDIA), B.Com (University of Mumbai)".

  two query terms matching yields 0.456.

  It is still correctly refused, because its cosine is 0.130 -- well under the
  0.30 floor. So this is the two gates doing exactly their job: a coincidental
  proper-noun match produced a mediocre lexical score, and the cosine signal
  caught the nonsense. Neither gate is redundant, and the residual risk of a
  rare-term coincidence is covered by requiring BOTH.

  The A6 statement query is a POSITIVE-SHAPED question that no source covers.
  It is correctly refused and must stay that way until the team decides on A6.

12. HOW TO RE-RUN AND VERIFY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  source .venv/bin/activate

  python -m src.cli ask "exit load on HDFC Flexi Cap Fund"
  python -m src.cli ask "What is HDFC Bank's FD interest rate?"    # expect refusal
  python -m src.cli ask "What is HDFC Bank's FD interest rate?" --explain
  python -m src.cli inspect
  pytest -q                                                       # 110 pass
  pytest -m "not llm and not integration"                         # 87, ~0.7s

  Confirm both gates are live and configured:

  python -c "
  from src.rag.retriever import Retriever
  r = Retriever()
  print('cosine gate  :', r.cfg.min_similarity)
  print('coverage gate:', r.cfg.min_lexical_coverage)
  print('index size   :', r.index_size)
  print('below both   :', r.search('Tell me about Bitcoin.') == [])
  print('above both   :', len(r.search('expense ratio of HDFC Large Cap Fund')) > 0)"

  Re-verify the separation gap at any time -- this is the number to watch if the
  corpus ever changes:

  python -c "
  from src.rag.retriever import Retriever
  r = Retriever()
  a = [max(x['coverage'] for x in r.explain(q)) for q in
       ['expense ratio of HDFC Large Cap Fund','exit load on HDFC Flexi Cap Fund',
        'minimum SIP amount','lock-in period HDFC ELSS',
        'benchmark of HDFC Balanced Advantage','riskometer level HDFC Small Cap']]
  n = [max(x['coverage'] for x in r.explain(q)) for q in
       ['how to download capital gains statement','What is HDFC Bank FD interest rate?',
        'Which fund is best for my portfolio?','What is the weather in Mumbai?',
        'Tell me about Bitcoin.','What is the price of gold today?']]
  print(f'answerable min {min(a):.3f} | refuse max {max(n):.3f} | gap {min(a)-max(n):+.3f}')
  assert min(a) > max(n), 'SETS NO LONGER SEPARATE -- re-tune'"

  Expect: answerable min 0.637 | refuse max 0.494 | gap +0.143
